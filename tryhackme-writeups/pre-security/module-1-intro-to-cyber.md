# Module: Introduction to Cyber Security — TryHackMe Pre Security

This module covered the two core "sides" of cybersecurity work, plus the range of career paths in the field.

## What I learned

- **Offensive security** is the "attacker" side — pentesters, red teamers, and ethical hackers who simulate real attacks to find weaknesses before malicious actors do. It's proactive: the goal is breaking in on purpose, with permission, to expose gaps.
- **Defensive security** is the flip side — SOC analysts, incident responders, and blue teamers who monitor systems, detect intrusions, and respond when something goes wrong. It's reactive/protective rather than proactive, though good defensive teams also do proactive threat hunting.
- The distinction clicked for me once I thought of it like a house: offensive security is someone hired to try every door and window to see what's unlocked; defensive security is the person watching the cameras and responding when someone tries to get in.
- **Careers in cyber** span far wider than I expected — it's not just "hacker" as a job title. Roles range from SOC analyst and incident responder (defensive), to pentester and red teamer (offensive), to governance/compliance/risk roles that are less technical but still central to security.

## Where I'm headed

Based on this module, I'm leaning defensive/SOC-analyst track to start. I plan to start from here and learn my way up. It's in line with my career goals.

## What confused me

To be honest,  I conflated  "defensive security" with just "IT support with extra steps". If you're not careful, you'd think that they are all-in-one but with the help of this module, I clarified that SOC/defensive work is genuinely its own discipline (log analysis, threat detection, incident response) rather than general troubleshooting.
